# 1009, LTD — Conflict of Interest Management Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Conflict of interest"** means a situation in which the Company's interests, the interests of a member of its management body, an employee, or a related party, materially conflict with the interests of a client or with the interests of one client compared to another.

1.3. **"Inducement"** means any fee, commission or non-monetary benefit paid or provided to, or by, any party other than the client in connection with a crypto-asset service.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements Article 72 of MiCA, which requires CASPs to implement and maintain effective arrangements to identify, prevent, manage and disclose conflicts of interest between themselves, including their managers, employees and any person directly or indirectly linked to them by control, and their clients, or between one client and another.

### Scope

2.2. This Policy applies to all crypto-asset services provided by the Company and binds all members of the management body, employees, contractors and any person acting on behalf of the Company.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- MiCA Art. 72 — Conflicts of interest.
- MiCA Art. 68 — Governance arrangements (general).
- ESMA Regulatory Technical Standards on conflicts of interest under MiCA.

## 3. Identification of Conflicts

3.1. The Company maintains an inventory of conflict-of-interest scenarios specific to its authorised services. The inventory includes at minimum, but is not limited to:

| Scenario | Affected service | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Proprietary trading against client orders | Execution, exchange | Strict separation of proprietary and client books; pre-trade transparency |
| Listing of an issuer related to the Company on a trading platform | Operation of a trading platform | Listing committee independence; written rationale; disclosure |
| Inducements from issuers or third parties | Placing, advice | Prohibition or disclosure; ex-ante client consent |
| Personal account dealing by employees in listed crypto-assets | All | Personal dealing pre-clearance; quiet periods |
| Performance-based remuneration linked to volume of a specific service | All | Remuneration aligned to long-term client outcomes; conflicts review |
| Cross-services use of client information | All | Information barriers; "need-to-know" access controls |
| Family or financial relationships with clients, counterparties, listed issuers | All | Declaration of interests; recusal from related decisions |

3.2. The inventory is reviewed at least annually and following any new authorised service, material business change, or relevant supervisory communication.

## 4. Prevention and Organisational Arrangements

4.1. The Company establishes the following organisational arrangements designed to prevent conflicts from harming client interests:

- **Information barriers** between functions whose unrestricted information flow could give rise to a conflict (e.g., proprietary trading desk and client execution desk; listing function and trading function).
- **Independent reporting lines** for control functions (Compliance, Risk, Internal Audit, AML/CFT) per **F01 Governance & Senior Management Accountability Policy**.
- **Personal account dealing rules** — all employees with access to material non-public information are subject to a Personal Account Dealing Procedure requiring pre-clearance, declaration of holdings, and observance of restricted/quiet periods.
- **Remuneration arrangements** that do not directly reward conduct that would generate conflicts (e.g., variable remuneration based solely on transaction volume of a single product).
- **Outside business activities** of management body members and employees require prior written approval.

## 5. Management of Conflicts that Cannot Be Prevented

5.1. Where organisational arrangements are not sufficient to ensure with reasonable confidence that the risk of damage to client interests will be prevented, the Company shall:

- Clearly disclose to the client, in good time before undertaking the relevant business, the general nature and sources of the conflict, the steps taken to mitigate it, and the residual risks (MiCA Art. 72(3)).
- Communicate the disclosure in a durable medium and with sufficient detail, taking into account the nature of the client, to enable an informed decision.

5.2. Disclosure is treated as a **measure of last resort** and not as a substitute for prevention.

## 6. Inducements

6.1. The Company shall not pay or accept any fee, commission or non-monetary benefit in connection with the provision of a crypto-asset service to a client unless:

- The payment or benefit is designed to enhance the quality of the service to the client.
- It does not impair compliance with the Company's duty to act honestly, fairly and professionally in the best interest of the client.
- It is clearly disclosed to the client prior to the provision of the service.

6.2. Minor non-monetary benefits that are reasonable, proportionate and of such a scale that they are unlikely to influence the Company's behaviour to the detriment of the client may be accepted without per-instance disclosure, provided they are clearly disclosed on a generic basis.

## 7. Register of Conflicts

7.1. The Company maintains a **Register of Conflicts** recording, for each identified conflict:

- Date of identification.
- Nature and sources.
- Functions, services, clients and individuals affected.
- Prevention and management measures applied.
- Any disclosure made and its evidence.
- Reviewer, approver and review date.

7.2. The Register is reviewed by the Compliance Officer at minimum quarterly and reported in summary form to the management body in its supervisory function.

## 8. Declarations of Interest

8.1. Members of the management body and employees in conflict-sensitive roles shall complete a Declaration of Interests at appointment, at least annually thereafter, and on the occurrence of any material change. Declarations cover, at minimum:

- Personal holdings of crypto-assets and financial instruments.
- Holdings of close family members where relevant.
- Outside business activities and directorships.
- Material gifts and entertainment received.

8.2. Declarations are reviewed by the Compliance Officer. Material concerns are escalated to the Chair of the management body in its supervisory function.

## 9. Training and Awareness

9.1. All employees receive conflict-of-interest training at onboarding and at least annually thereafter. Training records are retained for five (5) years.

## 10. Review

10.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following any new authorised service, any material conflict event, or the entry into force of new Level 2 measures under MiCA Art. 72.
