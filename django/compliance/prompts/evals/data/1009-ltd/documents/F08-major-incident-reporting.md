# 1009, LTD — Major ICT Incident Classification & Reporting Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"ICT-related incident"** means a single event or a series of linked events unplanned by the Company that compromises the security of the network and information systems and has an adverse impact on the availability, authenticity, integrity or confidentiality of data, or on the services provided (DORA Art. 3(8)).

1.3. **"Major ICT-related incident"** means an ICT-related incident that has a high adverse impact on the network and information systems supporting CIFs (DORA Art. 3(10)).

1.4. **"Significant cyber threat"** has the meaning given in DORA Art. 3(13).

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements the classification and reporting obligations of DORA Articles 17 to 23 in respect of ICT-related incidents and significant cyber threats. It complements **DOC 07 Incident Management Policy**, which sets out the operational mechanics of detection, response and recovery, by adding the regulatory classification thresholds and the templates, timelines and channels for NCA reporting.

### Scope

2.2. This Policy applies to all ICT-related incidents detected at the Company or notified to the Company by an ICT third-party service provider that have, or may reasonably be expected to have, an adverse impact on the Company's services or its CIFs.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- DORA Art. 17 — ICT-related incident management process.
- DORA Art. 18 — Classification of ICT-related incidents and cyber threats.
- DORA Art. 19 — Reporting of major ICT-related incidents and voluntary notification of significant cyber threats.
- DORA Art. 20 — Harmonisation of reporting content and templates.
- DORA Art. 23 — Operational or security payment-related incidents (where applicable to the Company's services).
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772 — RTS on classification criteria for major ICT-related incidents.
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/302 (and successor instruments) — ITS on reporting templates, timelines and channels.

## 3. Classification Criteria

3.1. The Company classifies each ICT-related incident against the seven (7) classification criteria laid down in DORA Art. 18 and the supporting RTS:

| # | Criterion | Indicators |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clients, financial counterparties and transactions affected | Number, identification, materiality, share of client base or transaction volume |
| 2 | Reputational impact | Media coverage; complaints; regulatory enquiries; public disclosure |
| 3 | Duration and service downtime | Time from detection to recovery; rolling thresholds for CIFs |
| 4 | Geographical spread | Member States affected by the incident's impact |
| 5 | Data losses | Availability, authenticity, integrity, confidentiality losses |
| 6 | Criticality of services affected | Whether CIFs are affected; nature and importance of the affected service |
| 7 | Economic impact | Direct and indirect costs and losses, both absolute and relative |

3.2. An incident is classified as **major** when the materiality thresholds defined in the RTS (Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1772) are met. The Risk Officer, supported by the Information Security Officer, owns the classification decision.

3.3. Classification is reassessed continuously as new information becomes available. Where reassessment changes the classification, the reporting timeline is recalibrated from the most recent classification determination but does not restart the underlying detection clock.

## 4. Significant Cyber Threats

4.1. A cyber threat is classified as **significant** based on the criteria in DORA Art. 18(2) and the supporting RTS, considering the potential impact on the Company's ICT systems, the probability of materialisation, and the technical characteristics of the threat.

4.2. The Company shall, on a voluntary basis, notify the National Competent Authority of significant cyber threats that it considers of relevance to the financial system (DORA Art. 19(2)).

## 5. Reporting Workflow

5.1. The reporting workflow for major ICT-related incidents follows a three-stage cadence:

| Report | Trigger | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial notification** | Classification of an incident as major | As soon as possible, and no later than the timing specified by the ITS |
| **Intermediate report** | Material change to the incident, its impact, or relevant new information | At the cadence specified by the ITS, including at minimum a "status known" update |
| **Final report** | Root cause analysis completed and recovery sustained | No later than the timing specified by the ITS following the root-cause analysis closure |

5.2. The Company uses the standardised reporting templates set out in the applicable ITS. Reports are submitted to the National Competent Authority through the channel designated by it.

5.3. The Compliance Officer is responsible for the timely submission of all reports and for liaison with the NCA throughout the lifecycle of the incident.

## 6. Cross-Notifications

6.1. Where the incident is likely to have adverse effects on the financial interests of clients, the Company shall, without undue delay, inform affected clients and, where applicable, of any reasonable measures they may take to mitigate the adverse effects (DORA Art. 19(3)).

6.2. Where the incident has implications under other legal regimes (notably Regulation (EU) 2016/679 in respect of personal data, the eIDAS Regulation, or NIS2 in respect of essential or important entities where applicable), the Company shall coordinate notifications to the relevant authorities in alignment with the applicable timelines.

## 7. Internal Escalation

7.1. The internal escalation flow is as follows:

- **Detection** by Security Operations or by automated controls → ticket opened.
- **Triage** by the Information Security Officer within agreed SLOs.
- **Classification** by the Risk Officer where the incident may meet major-incident thresholds.
- **Crisis activation** where classification is major, with the Crisis Management Team convened by the Risk Officer.
- **Management body notification** within the timelines defined in **F01 Governance & Senior Management Accountability Policy**.

## 8. Root-Cause Analysis and Post-Incident Review

8.1. Following the closure of a major ICT-related incident, the Company conducts a structured post-incident review covering:

- Timeline reconstruction.
- Root-cause analysis (technical and process).
- Effectiveness of detection, response and communication.
- Identification of preventive, detective and corrective actions.
- Lessons learned that feed back into the ICT risk management framework, testing programme, and policies.

8.2. The review is documented and approved by the Risk Officer. Material lessons are reported to the management body in its supervisory function.

## 9. Aggregation Rule

9.1. Where two or more incidents share the same root cause or are otherwise linked, they are aggregated for classification purposes in accordance with the criteria of the applicable RTS.

## 10. Record-Keeping

10.1. The Company retains for at least five (5) years:

- All ICT-related incident records, including non-major incidents.
- Classification rationales and reassessments.
- All NCA notifications and reports, with submission evidence.
- Cross-notifications to other authorities and clients.
- Root-cause analyses and post-incident review files.

## 11. Review

11.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following any major ICT-related incident, any change to the Level 2/Level 3 measures under DORA Chapter III, or any material change in the Company's authorised services or ICT estate.
