# 1009, LTD — Market Abuse Prevention Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Inside information"** has the meaning given in MiCA Art. 87(1): information of a precise nature that has not been made public, relating, directly or indirectly, to one or more issuers of crypto-assets or to one or more crypto-assets, and which, if it were made public, would be likely to have a significant effect on the prices of those crypto-assets or on the price of related crypto-assets.

1.3. **"Insider dealing"**, **"unlawful disclosure of inside information"**, **"market manipulation"** have the meanings given in MiCA Art. 89, 90 and 91 respectively.

1.4. **"STOR"** means a Suspicious Transaction or Order Report submitted to the National Competent Authority under MiCA Art. 92.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements Title VI of MiCA (Articles 86 to 92) prohibiting market abuse in relation to crypto-assets admitted to trading on the Company's trading platform, and establishes the surveillance, escalation and reporting arrangements through which the Company detects and reports prohibited conduct.

### Scope

2.2. This Policy applies to:

- All crypto-assets admitted to trading on the Company's trading platform (where applicable).
- All trading activity executed on, or transmitted through, the Company.
- All employees, management body members, contractors and other persons acting on behalf of the Company.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- MiCA Art. 86 — Scope of market abuse rules for crypto-assets.
- MiCA Art. 87 — Inside information.
- MiCA Art. 88 — Public disclosure of inside information.
- MiCA Art. 89 — Prohibition of insider dealing.
- MiCA Art. 90 — Prohibition of unlawful disclosure of inside information.
- MiCA Art. 91 — Prohibition of market manipulation.
- MiCA Art. 92 — Prevention and detection of market abuse.
- ESMA Regulatory and Implementing Technical Standards on systems and templates for STOR submissions.

## 3. Prohibited Conduct

3.1. The Company prohibits, and shall take all reasonable steps to detect and report, the following conduct in respect of crypto-assets:

- **Insider dealing** — acquiring or disposing of crypto-assets on the basis of inside information, or recommending or inducing another person to do so.
- **Unlawful disclosure** — disclosing inside information except where the disclosure is made in the normal exercise of an employment, profession or duties.
- **Market manipulation**, including but not limited to:
- Wash trades and matched orders.
- Painting the tape and layering / spoofing.
- Pump-and-dump schemes coordinated through social media or other channels.
- Dissemination of false or misleading information through any media, including online platforms.
- Distortion of benchmarks or reference prices used for crypto-assets.

## 4. Surveillance

4.1. The Company operates a surveillance programme covering:

- Order book activity on the Company's trading platform (where applicable).
- Client transactions executed by the Company.
- Public information sources, including issuer disclosures and significant social-media signals affecting listed crypto-assets.

4.2. The surveillance programme uses automated alerting calibrated to the trading characteristics of crypto-assets, complemented by analyst review. Alert scenarios include, at minimum:

| Scenario | Description |
|---|---|
| Wash trading | Same beneficial owner on both sides of a trade |
| Matched orders | Pre-arranged trading between counterparties |
| Layering / spoofing | Placement of orders without intention to execute, to mislead other participants |
| Front-running | Trading ahead of a known imminent client order |
| Insider trading proxy | Unusual trading pattern preceding material public disclosure by an issuer |
| Marking the close | Anomalous trading near reference-price windows |
| Cross-product / cross-venue manipulation | Coordinated activity across venues to move prices |
| Social-media driven pump-and-dump | Anomalous order book activity correlated to coordinated public discourse |

4.3. Alerts are triaged within agreed SLOs. Confirmed suspicions are escalated to the Compliance Officer for STOR determination.

## 5. Public Disclosure of Inside Information

5.1. Where the Company is itself in possession of inside information that directly concerns the Company (acting as issuer or otherwise), it shall disclose that information to the public as soon as possible in accordance with MiCA Art. 88.

5.2. The Company may delay disclosure in the limited circumstances permitted by MiCA Art. 88(2). Any delay must be documented at the time of the decision, including the legitimate interests at stake, the absence of risk of misleading the public, and the existence of effective confidentiality measures.

5.3. The Company maintains an **Insider List** in the format prescribed by the applicable ITS, recording all persons with access to specific inside information.

## 6. Suspicious Transaction or Order Reports (STORs)

6.1. Where the Compliance Officer has reasonable grounds to suspect that a transaction or order constitutes insider dealing or market manipulation (or attempts thereto), the Company shall, without delay, file a STOR with the National Competent Authority.

6.2. STORs are filed using the standardised template prescribed by the applicable ITS and submitted through the channel designated by the NCA.

6.3. The Company shall not disclose the existence or content of a STOR to the subject of the report or to any person other than those authorised to receive it.

## 7. Personal Account Dealing

7.1. Members of the management body and employees with access to inside information shall comply with the Personal Account Dealing Procedure, which requires pre-clearance of trades in crypto-assets, prohibits dealing during defined restricted periods, and requires periodic disclosure of holdings.

7.2. The Personal Account Dealing Procedure is referenced and complemented by **F04 Conflict of Interest Management Policy**.

## 8. Training and Awareness

8.1. All employees and members of the management body receive market-abuse training at onboarding and at least annually thereafter. Surveillance, Compliance and trading-floor personnel receive specialised training appropriate to their function. Records are retained for five (5) years.

## 9. Record-Keeping

9.1. The Company retains for at least five (5) years:

- Surveillance alert logs and disposition decisions.
- STOR filings and supporting evidence.
- Insider lists and amendments thereto.
- Personal account dealing approvals and declarations.
- Records of delayed-disclosure decisions and rationales.

## 10. Review

10.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following any change in the Company's trading platform operation, any material STOR filing, NCA feedback, or the entry into force of new Level 2/Level 3 measures under MiCA Title VI.
