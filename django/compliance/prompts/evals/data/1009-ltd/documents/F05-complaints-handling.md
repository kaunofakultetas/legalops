# 1009, LTD — Complaints Handling Procedure

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Complaint"** means a statement of dissatisfaction addressed to the Company by a client or potential client relating to the provision of a crypto-asset service. A simple request for information, clarification or correction of an obvious error is not a complaint.

1.3. **"Complainant"** means a natural or legal person presumed to be eligible to have a complaint considered by the Company.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Procedure implements Article 71 of MiCA, which requires CASPs to establish and maintain effective and transparent procedures for the prompt, fair and consistent handling of complaints received from clients, and to publish a description of those procedures.

### Scope

2.2. This Procedure applies to all complaints relating to crypto-asset services provided by the Company, whether received from existing clients, prospective clients, or third parties whose interests are directly affected by such services.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- MiCA Art. 71 — Complaints-handling procedures.
- ESMA Regulatory Technical Standards specifying complaints-handling requirements under MiCA.
- Out-of-court dispute resolution mechanisms applicable in the Republic of Lithuania.

## 3. Channels for Submitting Complaints

3.1. Complaints may be submitted to the Company free of charge through any of the following channels:

- A dedicated complaints web form available on the Company's website.
- The email address **complaints@1009.lt**.
- Postal mail addressed to the registered office of the Company, marked "Complaints".
- In person at the Company's premises during business hours.

3.2. Complaints may be submitted in any official language of the European Union in which the Company markets its services, and in Lithuanian and English.

3.3. The Company does not require complainants to pay any fee for submitting or processing a complaint.

## 4. Acknowledgement and Investigation

4.1. The Company shall acknowledge receipt of a complaint in a durable medium within **two (2) business days** of receipt.

4.2. The acknowledgement shall include:

- A unique complaint reference number.
- The name and contact details of the person handling the complaint.
- An indication of the expected timeline for substantive reply.
- Information on out-of-court redress mechanisms available to the complainant.

4.3. The Company shall investigate complaints competently, diligently and impartially, obtaining additional information as necessary, free of charge to the complainant.

## 5. Response Timeline

5.1. The Company shall provide a substantive reply to the complainant **no later than fifteen (15) business days** from receipt of the complaint.

5.2. In exceptional circumstances where a reply within fifteen business days is not possible for reasons beyond the Company's control, the Company shall:

- Send a holding response clearly stating the reasons for the delay.
- Specify the timeframe within which the complainant will receive a substantive reply, which shall not exceed thirty-five (35) business days from receipt of the complaint.

## 6. Substantive Reply

6.1. The substantive reply is communicated in a durable medium and in plain, intelligible language. It addresses each of the points raised in the complaint and:

- States the outcome of the investigation.
- Where the complaint is upheld in whole or in part, sets out the remedies offered (including any compensation, fee refund or service restoration).
- Where the complaint is rejected in whole or in part, provides a clear explanation of the Company's position and the reasoning.
- Informs the complainant of the right to refer the complaint to an out-of-court dispute resolution body or to the competent court, and provides the relevant contact information.

## 7. Internal Organisation

7.1. The Company designates a **Complaints Management Function** under the responsibility of the Compliance Officer. The function:

- Operates independently of the business units whose conduct is under review.
- Maintains the complaints register and produces management information.
- Identifies recurring or systemic issues and proposes corrective measures.

7.2. Employees handling complaints have sufficient knowledge, time and authority to investigate complaints fairly and reach an informed decision.

## 8. Register of Complaints

8.1. The Company maintains a Register of Complaints recording, for each complaint:

- Unique reference number; date of receipt and date of closure.
- Identity of the complainant and nature of the relationship to the Company.
- Subject matter and root cause analysis.
- Service or product affected.
- Outcome (upheld, partially upheld, rejected) and remedies.
- Any subsequent escalation to an out-of-court body or court.

8.2. The Register is reviewed at minimum quarterly by the Compliance Officer for root-cause patterns. Material issues are escalated to the management body and feed back into product, training and control improvements.

8.3. Records are retained for at least five (5) years.

## 9. Reporting to the Management Body and NCA

9.1. The Compliance Officer reports to the management body in its supervisory function, at minimum quarterly, on:

- The total volume of complaints and trends.
- The principal subject matters of complaints.
- Average resolution times and breaches of internal service levels.
- Root-cause findings and remedial actions.

9.2. Information on complaints is provided to the National Competent Authority upon request and on the periodic basis required by applicable Level 2/Level 3 measures.

## 10. Public Disclosure

10.1. The Company publishes on its website a description of the complaints-handling procedure that includes the channels for submission, the timelines applicable, the right to refer to an out-of-court dispute resolution body, and the relevant contact details.

## 11. Review

11.1. This Procedure is reviewed at least annually and following any material change in service offering, supervisory communication, or the entry into force of new Level 2 measures under MiCA Art. 71.
