# 1009, LTD — Governance & Senior Management Accountability Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Management body"** means the body or bodies of 1009 appointed in accordance with national law, which are empowered to set the Company's strategy, objectives and overall direction, and which oversee and monitor management decision-making, including persons who effectively direct the business of the Company (MiCA Art. 3(1)(27)).

1.3. **"Management body in its supervisory function"** has the meaning given in DORA Art. 3(7) and refers to the body, or members of the body, performing supervisory functions in respect of management decisions taken by the executive members.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy sets the governance arrangements through which the management body of 1009 directs, monitors and is accountable for the Company's compliance with the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) and the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA). It implements the explicit accountability requirements placed on the management body by Article 68 of MiCA and Article 5 of DORA.

2.2. The Policy operationalises the principle that governance of crypto-asset services and of ICT risk is a board-level responsibility that cannot be delegated to operational management or to third-party providers.

### Scope

2.3. This Policy binds all members of the management body in both executive and supervisory functions, senior management, the Compliance Officer, the Risk Officer, the Internal Audit function, and all employees with delegated decision authority over crypto-asset services or ICT risk.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.4. Primary anchors:

- MiCA Art. 68 — Governance arrangements for CASPs.
- MiCA Art. 68(7) — Fitness and propriety of members of the management body and shareholders/members with qualifying holdings.
- DORA Art. 5 — Governance and organisation; ultimate responsibility of the management body for ICT risk.
- DORA Art. 6 — ICT risk management framework approval.
- EBA/ESMA Joint Guidelines on the suitability assessment of members of the management body under MiCA.

## 3. Management Body Responsibilities

3.1. The management body bears **ultimate and full** responsibility for the Company's compliance with MiCA and DORA. This responsibility is non-delegable and includes, at minimum, the duties set out in this Section 3.

### Strategic Direction

3.2. The management body shall:

- Approve the Company's business strategy and risk appetite, including the scope of authorised crypto-asset services.
- Approve the digital operational resilience strategy and the ICT risk management framework (DORA Art. 6).
- Set the ICT risk tolerance level in alignment with the Company's risk appetite.
- Approve and periodically review all policies in the FinTech series (F01–F12) and the sector-agnostic policies (DOC 01–12).

### Oversight and Challenge

3.3. The management body in its supervisory function shall:

- Receive at minimum quarterly reporting on ICT risk, ICT-related incidents, third-party arrangements and digital operational resilience testing outcomes.
- Challenge management on the adequacy and effectiveness of ICT risk controls.
- Approve the Register of Information of contractual arrangements with ICT third-party service providers (DORA Art. 28(3)) before its submission to the National Competent Authority (NCA).

### Resources and Competence

3.4. The management body shall ensure that:

- Sufficient budgetary and human resources are allocated to ICT risk management, compliance, and the internal audit function.
- Members of the management body collectively and individually possess and continuously maintain adequate knowledge, skills and experience to understand ICT risk and its impact on the Company's operations (DORA Art. 5(4)).
- Mandatory ICT risk-related training is delivered to all members of the management body at least annually.

## 4. Fitness and Propriety

4.1. Each member of the management body and each holder of a qualifying holding (≥10% of capital or voting rights) shall satisfy the fitness and propriety requirements set out in MiCA Art. 68(7) and MiCA Art. 84.

4.2. The assessment covers, at minimum:

- **Reputation, honesty and integrity** — absence of criminal convictions for offences relevant to financial services, AML/CFT, or market abuse; no record of regulatory sanctions.
- **Knowledge and experience** — collective and individual competence in crypto-asset services, financial regulation, ICT risk and governance.
- **Independence of mind** — ability to take decisions independently of conflicts of interest.
- **Time commitment** — sufficient time devoted to the function, with reasonable limits on concurrent directorships.

4.3. The Company maintains a documented suitability assessment for each member at appointment and reassesses at minimum annually and upon any material change in circumstances. Records are retained for ten (10) years and made available to the NCA upon request.

## 5. Roles and Responsibilities

5.1. The Company establishes the following control functions, each reporting independently to the management body:

| Function | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| Compliance Officer | Monitor MiCA/DORA compliance; advise management body; maintain regulatory horizon scanning; coordinate NCA interactions. |
| Risk Officer | Operate the ICT risk management framework; aggregate risk reporting to the management body; coordinate DORT testing. |
| Internal Audit | Independent assurance over the design and effectiveness of ICT risk controls; minimum annual audit plan approved by the management body. |
| AML/CFT Officer | Discharge obligations under the TFR and AML legislation (see F10). |
| Information Security Officer | Operational ownership of ICT security controls; reports incidents and KRIs to the Risk Officer. |

5.2. The Compliance Officer, Risk Officer, AML/CFT Officer and Internal Audit functions are organisationally separated from each other and from operational/revenue-generating units. The Compliance and AML/CFT functions report to the management body without filter through executive management.

## 6. Conflicts within the Management Body

6.1. Members of the management body shall identify, declare and where possible avoid personal conflicts of interest. Where a conflict cannot be avoided, the member shall abstain from voting on the affected matter and the abstention shall be recorded in the minutes.

6.2. Detailed handling of conflicts of interest at the Company level is set out in **F04 Conflict of Interest Management Policy**.

## 7. Record-Keeping and NCA Reporting

7.1. The Company maintains for at least five (5) years (or longer where required by national law):

- Minutes of all meetings of the management body and its committees.
- Records of strategic, risk, and compliance decisions taken.
- Suitability assessments for each member.
- Approvals of all policies in the FinTech series and material amendments thereto.

7.2. The Company notifies the NCA of:

- Any change in the composition of the management body, prior to the change taking effect (MiCA Art. 68(7)).
- Any acquisition or disposal of a qualifying holding, in accordance with MiCA Art. 84.
- Any material change to its governance arrangements.

## 8. Review

8.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following any of the events listed in **F00 Section 7.1**. The Compliance Officer prepares the review; the management body in its supervisory function approves it.
