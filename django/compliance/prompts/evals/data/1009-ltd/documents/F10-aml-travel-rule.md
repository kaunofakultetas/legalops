# 1009, LTD — AML/CFT and Travel Rule Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Transfer of crypto-assets"** has the meaning given in Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 ("TFR") Art. 3(10).

1.3. **"Originator"** and **"Beneficiary"** have the meanings given in TFR Art. 3(6) and 3(8) respectively.

1.4. **"Self-hosted address"** means a distributed ledger address that is not held by or registered with a crypto-asset service provider (TFR Art. 3(20)).

1.5. **"Travel Rule data"** means the information on originator and beneficiary required to accompany a transfer of crypto-assets under the TFR.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements:

- The AML/CFT obligations of the Company as an obliged entity under the applicable Union and national AML legislation; and
- The information accompanying transfers of crypto-assets obligations of Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 (the "Travel Rule").

2.2. The Policy operates in conjunction with the Company's customer due diligence (CDD), transaction monitoring, sanctions screening and suspicious-transaction reporting procedures.

### Scope

2.3. This Policy applies to:

- All business relationships and occasional transactions entered into by the Company.
- All transfers of crypto-assets to or from the Company on behalf of clients, regardless of value.
- All employees, contractors and any person performing AML-relevant tasks on behalf of the Company.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.4. Primary anchors:

- Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 — Travel Rule for transfers of funds and certain crypto-assets.
- Directive (EU) 2015/849 (AMLD5) and the AMLR/AMLD6 package (Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 and Directive (EU) 2024/1640).
- Regulation (EU) 2024/1620 establishing the Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA).
- National AML/CFT legislation of the Republic of Lithuania and guidance of the Financial Crime Investigation Service (FNTT).
- EBA Guidelines on customer due diligence and other AML-relevant guidelines applicable to CASPs.

## 3. Governance

3.1. The Company appoints an **AML/CFT Officer** at senior management level, with sufficient authority, independence, expertise and resources, and with direct reporting access to the management body.

3.2. The management body approves this Policy, the Business-Wide Risk Assessment (BWRA), the Group AML/CFT framework where applicable, and material amendments to any of the above.

## 4. Business-Wide Risk Assessment

4.1. The Company maintains an up-to-date Business-Wide Risk Assessment identifying and assessing the money-laundering and terrorist-financing risks to which the Company is exposed, considering at minimum:

- Client risk (segment, distribution channel, behaviour).
- Geographic risk (Member States and third countries, including high-risk third countries).
- Product and service risk (each authorised crypto-asset service).
- Transaction risk (size, frequency, counterparties, use of self-hosted addresses, privacy-enhancing features).
- Delivery channel risk.

4.2. The BWRA is reviewed at minimum annually and following any material change in business or risk landscape.

## 5. Customer Due Diligence

5.1. The Company applies CDD measures, in scope and depth proportionate to the assessed risk, at the times prescribed by applicable AML legislation, including:

- Establishment of a business relationship.
- Occasional transactions exceeding applicable thresholds.
- Any transfer of crypto-assets, regardless of value (per TFR).
- Suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing.
- Doubts about the veracity or adequacy of previously obtained client identification data.

5.2. CDD comprises:

- Identification and verification of the client and any beneficial owner using reliable and independent sources.
- Understanding of the nature and purpose of the business relationship.
- Ongoing monitoring of the business relationship and review of transactions.

5.3. **Enhanced Due Diligence** (EDD) is applied to higher-risk situations, including dealings with persons established in high-risk third countries, politically exposed persons (PEPs), and transactions or relationships presenting heightened ML/TF risk.

5.4. **Simplified Due Diligence** may be applied only where the Company has determined that the business relationship or transaction presents a lower degree of risk, and only to the extent permitted by applicable AML legislation.

## 6. Transaction Monitoring and Sanctions Screening

6.1. The Company performs:

- Continuous, automated monitoring of client transactions against rules calibrated to the BWRA outcomes.
- Real-time screening of clients and counterparties against EU consolidated sanctions lists, UN sanctions lists, and other applicable lists.
- Blockchain analytics to assess the risk of incoming and outgoing crypto-asset transfers, including exposure to high-risk addresses, mixers, sanctioned entities and illicit-activity clusters.

6.2. Alerts are triaged and disposed within agreed SLOs. Confirmed suspicions are reported to the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) without delay.

## 7. Travel Rule for Transfers of Crypto-Assets

7.1. **Outgoing transfers** — where the Company acts as the **originator's CASP**, it shall ensure that each transfer of crypto-assets is accompanied by the originator and beneficiary information required by TFR Art. 14, namely:

- Originator: name; distributed ledger address (or account number); address, official personal document number, customer identification number, or date and place of birth.
- Beneficiary: name; distributed ledger address (or account number).

7.2. The Company shall not execute a transfer where the required information is missing or incomplete.

7.3. **Incoming transfers** — where the Company acts as the **beneficiary's CASP**, it shall apply effective procedures to detect whether the required information is included with the transfer, and shall implement risk-based procedures to deal with transfers lacking required information, including suspension, return or rejection where appropriate.

7.4. **Self-hosted addresses** — for transfers to or from a self-hosted address, the Company shall apply the additional measures required by TFR Art. 14(5) and 16(2), including verification that the address is owned or controlled by the relevant client, particularly where the transfer exceeds the threshold defined in the TFR. The Company assesses and documents the level of ML/TF risk associated with each transfer involving a self-hosted address and applies enhanced measures where appropriate.

7.5. The Company maintains records of all Travel Rule data sent, received and any deficiencies detected, for at least five (5) years.

## 8. Suspicious Activity Reporting

8.1. Where the Company knows, suspects or has reasonable grounds to suspect that funds, regardless of the amount involved, are the proceeds of criminal activity or are related to terrorist financing, it shall:

- Promptly inform the FIU on its own initiative, in accordance with applicable national legislation.
- Refrain from carrying out transactions known or suspected to be related to ML/TF until the FIU has been informed and any required instruction received.
- Refrain from "tipping off" the client or any third party that information has been or will be transmitted to the FIU.

## 9. Record-Keeping

9.1. The Company retains for at least five (5) years (or longer where required by national law):

- CDD records, including copies of identification documents and verification evidence.
- Records of transactions sufficient to reconstruct each transaction.
- Travel Rule data and screening results.
- Internal and external suspicious-activity reports.
- BWRA versions and approval records.

## 10. Training

10.1. All employees receive AML/CFT and Travel Rule training at onboarding and at least annually thereafter. Training content is tailored to the employee's role and is updated to reflect material regulatory changes. Records are retained for five (5) years.

## 11. Independent Review

11.1. The Company subjects its AML/CFT framework to independent review at a frequency commensurate with the assessed risk, and at minimum every two (2) years. Findings are reported to the management body and remediated in accordance with the discipline of **F07 Digital Operational Resilience Testing Policy** Section 7.

## 12. Review

12.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following entry into application of any new AML/CFT instrument (notably AMLR/AMLD6 milestones and AMLA Level 2 measures), material change in the Company's risk profile, or supervisory finding.
