# 1009, LTD — Marketing Communications & Disclosures Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"Marketing communication"** means any disclosure of information, other than a crypto-asset white paper, advertising or promoting (a) an offer to the public of a crypto-asset, (b) the admission to trading of a crypto-asset on a trading platform, or (c) the crypto-asset services provided by the Company.

1.3. **"White paper"** means the document required by MiCA Title II, III or IV (as applicable) to be drawn up by an offeror or person seeking admission to trading.

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements the conduct-of-business and transparency obligations applicable to the Company's marketing communications and pre-contractual disclosures, including:

- Marketing-communication standards under MiCA Art. 7 (offers of crypto-assets), Art. 27 (asset-referenced tokens / ARTs) and Art. 53 (e-money tokens / EMTs).
- Information to be provided to clients of CASPs under MiCA Art. 66 (duty to act honestly, fairly and professionally) and the conduct-specific provisions of MiCA Art. 69 to 83.

### Scope

2.2. This Policy applies to all marketing communications and disclosures by or on behalf of the Company, in any medium (website, social media, paid advertising, sponsored content, influencer arrangements, email, push notification, in-app, print, broadcast, and in-person events).

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- MiCA Art. 6 to 9 — White paper for crypto-assets other than ARTs/EMTs (where applicable).
- MiCA Art. 7 — Marketing communications (crypto-assets other than ARTs/EMTs).
- MiCA Art. 26 to 29 — White paper and marketing communications for ARTs.
- MiCA Art. 51 to 53 — White paper and marketing communications for EMTs.
- MiCA Art. 66 — General duty to act honestly, fairly and professionally.
- MiCA Art. 69 to 83 — Specific information requirements per crypto-asset service.
- ESMA Guidelines on the conditions and criteria for the qualification of crypto-assets as financial instruments (for boundary cases).

## 3. Core Standards for Marketing Communications

3.1. All marketing communications shall:

- Be **clearly identifiable as such**.
- Be **fair, clear and not misleading**.
- Be **consistent** with the information in any applicable white paper.
- Include a **prominent warning** that the value of crypto-assets may fall as well as rise, and that investors may lose all or part of the amount invested.
- **Not** mislead potential holders by reference to envisaged features, performance, or services that have not been achieved or will not be made available.
- **Not** present any guarantee of future returns.

3.2. Where a white paper exists, the marketing communication shall include a clear statement of that fact and the address of the website where the white paper is published.

## 4. Pre-Contractual Information for Clients of CASPs

4.1. Before entering into a business relationship or providing a crypto-asset service, the Company shall provide the client, in good time and in a durable medium, with the information required by MiCA Art. 69, including:

- Identity of the Company and its contact details.
- A description of the crypto-asset service and the crypto-assets it concerns.
- All costs, fees and charges that will be passed to the client, including any taxes or stamp duties to be settled through the Company.
- The Company's complaint-handling procedure (see **F05 Complaints Handling Procedure**).
- The Company's conflict-of-interest arrangements (see **F04 Conflict of Interest Management Policy**).
- The Company's safeguarding arrangements for client funds and crypto-assets (see **F02** and **F03**).

4.2. Information shall be presented in a **fair, clear and not misleading** manner, in plain language and in the form required by the applicable rules.

## 5. Specific Service Disclosures

5.1. The Company shall provide additional service-specific information as required by:

- MiCA Art. 75 — Custody and administration: position statements; custody policy summary; segregation arrangements; liability regime.
- MiCA Art. 76 — Operation of a trading platform: trading rules; admission criteria; order types; market integrity arrangements.
- MiCA Art. 77, 78, 79 — Exchange, execution: best-execution arrangements; venue selection; pricing methodology.
- MiCA Art. 82, 83 — Advice, portfolio management: suitability assessment; risks of the crypto-assets considered.

## 6. White Paper (Where Applicable)

6.1. Where the Company offers a crypto-asset to the public or seeks admission to trading on its own account (rather than for a third-party issuer/offeror), it shall:

- Draw up the white paper in the format and with the content required by MiCA Art. 6 (or Art. 19 for ARTs, Art. 51 for EMTs).
- Submit the white paper to the National Competent Authority within the prescribed timeline.
- Publish the white paper on the Company's website upon notification to the NCA (or, for ARTs/EMTs, upon authorisation).
- Update the white paper whenever there is a significant new factor capable of affecting the assessment of the crypto-asset.

6.2. The white paper shall contain a clear and unambiguous statement that:

- The crypto-asset may lose its value in part or in full.
- The crypto-asset may not always be transferable.
- The crypto-asset may not be liquid.
- Where the crypto-asset is not an EMT or ART, it is not covered by investor compensation schemes or deposit guarantee schemes.

## 7. Approval and Recordkeeping Workflow

7.1. Every marketing communication, prior to first publication, shall be:

- Drafted by Marketing & Communications.
- Reviewed by Compliance against this Policy, MiCA conduct standards and (where relevant) the applicable white paper.
- Approved by the Compliance Officer or a delegate.

7.2. The Company maintains a Marketing Approvals Register recording, for each communication:

- Channel, audience and territories.
- Date of first publication and any subsequent material changes.
- Compliance reviewer and approver.
- Linkage to the underlying white paper where applicable.
- Date of withdrawal where the communication has been retired.

7.3. Records are retained for at least five (5) years.

## 8. Third-Party Distribution and Influencer Arrangements

8.1. Where the Company engages a third party (including agencies, affiliates and individual social-media communicators) to distribute or amplify marketing communications, the Company shall:

- Subject the arrangement to a written contract containing the relevant standards of this Policy.
- Provide pre-approved content; ad-hoc deviations require Compliance pre-approval.
- Require disclosure that the communication is paid promotion.
- Monitor compliance and retain records of monitoring outcomes.

## 9. Cross-Border Communications

9.1. Marketing communications targeted at clients in Member States other than Lithuania shall comply with the conduct rules and language requirements of the relevant host Member State, in addition to the rules of this Policy.

## 10. Withdrawal and Corrective Communications

10.1. Where a marketing communication is found to be unfair, unclear or misleading, the Company shall:

- Withdraw the communication without delay across all channels in which it was published.
- Where appropriate, publish a corrective communication of equivalent prominence.
- Investigate the root cause and update controls accordingly.
- Notify the National Competent Authority where required by applicable rules.

## 11. Review

11.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually and following any material change in the Company's service offering, any supervisory finding, or the entry into force of new Level 2/Level 3 measures relating to marketing communications and disclosures.
