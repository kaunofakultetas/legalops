# 1009, LTD — ICT Third-Party Risk Management Policy

## 1. Definitions

1.1. For a detailed list of term definitions used in this document, please refer to the Definitions and Abbreviations Glossary maintained by 1009, LTD.

1.2. **"ICT services"** has the meaning given in DORA Art. 3(21): digital and data services provided through ICT systems to one or more internal or external users on an ongoing basis, including hardware as a service and hardware services which include the provision of technical support via software or firmware updates by the hardware provider, excluding traditional analogue telephone services.

1.3. **"ICT third-party service provider"** has the meaning given in DORA Art. 3(19).

1.4. **"Critical or important function"** ("CIF") means a function the disruption of which would materially impair the financial performance of the Company, or the soundness or continuity of its services and activities, or the discontinued, defective or failed performance of that function would materially impair the continuing compliance of the Company with the conditions of its authorisation or its other obligations (DORA Art. 3(22)).

## 2. Context

### Purpose

2.1. This Policy implements the ICT third-party risk management framework required by DORA Articles 28 to 30 and complements MiCA Article 73 on outsourcing of operational functions to third parties.

### Scope

2.2. This Policy applies to all contractual arrangements between the Company and ICT third-party service providers, regardless of whether the provider is an intra-group entity or an external entity, and irrespective of contract type.

### Legal and Regulatory Framework

2.3. Primary anchors:

- DORA Art. 28 — General principles.
- DORA Art. 29 — Preliminary assessment of ICT concentration risk.
- DORA Art. 30 — Key contractual provisions.
- DORA Art. 31–44 — Oversight framework for critical ICT third-party service providers (informational; the Company is supervised, not the provider).
- Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956 — Standard templates for the Register of Information.
- Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1773 — RTS specifying detailed content of the policy on the use of ICT services supporting critical or important functions.
- MiCA Art. 73 — Outsourcing.

## 3. Governance

3.1. The management body retains ultimate responsibility for ICT third-party arrangements (DORA Art. 5(2)(g)) and shall:

- Approve and review this Policy and material amendments thereto.
- Approve the strategy on ICT third-party risk, including the multi-vendor strategy where applicable.
- Approve the Register of Information before its submission to the National Competent Authority.
- Approve contractual arrangements supporting critical or important functions and any material change thereto.

3.2. The Risk Officer maintains the operational ownership of this Policy. The Procurement function executes the contracting process under risk and compliance oversight.

## 4. Lifecycle Management

### Pre-Contractual Phase

4.1. Before entering into any contractual arrangement with an ICT third-party service provider, the Company shall:

- Assess whether the arrangement covers a critical or important function (CIF).
- Identify and assess all relevant risks, including ICT, operational, legal, reputational, and concentration risks.
- Perform a due diligence on the provider proportionate to the criticality of the arrangement, covering at minimum: financial soundness; security posture (independent attestations, e.g., SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001); business continuity and resilience capabilities; sub-outsourcing chain; geographic location of data processing and applicable legal regime.
- Perform a concentration risk assessment in accordance with DORA Art. 29, considering use of the same provider for multiple CIFs, difficulty of substitutability, and dependency of the Company's business model.

4.2. Where the function is critical or important, the Compliance Officer issues an opinion on regulatory compatibility, and the management body in its supervisory function approves the engagement.

### Contractual Phase

4.3. All contractual arrangements shall be in writing and incorporate the elements required by DORA Art. 30(2). Arrangements supporting CIFs shall additionally include the elements of DORA Art. 30(3), including:

- Description of all functions and ICT services provided, and indication of permitted sub-contracting.
- Locations where data is processed and stored, and notification obligations on relocation.
- Provisions on availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data.
- Service levels, including precise quantitative and qualitative performance targets.
- Obligation to assist the Company in case of an ICT incident at no additional cost or at a pre-determined cost.
- Cooperation with the National Competent Authority and resolution authorities.
- Termination rights and notice periods.
- Audit and access rights for the Company, its auditors, and the National Competent Authority.
- Exit strategies, including transition periods and assistance.
- Participation in the Company's ICT security awareness programmes and digital operational resilience training.

### Ongoing Monitoring

4.4. The Company shall monitor each arrangement throughout its lifecycle. Monitoring for arrangements supporting CIFs includes, at minimum:

- Service-level performance against contractual targets.
- Security incidents at the provider and their impact assessment.
- Independent attestation refresh cycles.
- Changes in sub-contracting chains, ownership or financial soundness of the provider.
- Periodic risk reassessment (at minimum annually).

### Exit Strategy

4.5. For each arrangement supporting a CIF, the Company maintains a documented exit strategy that:

- Is realistic, feasible and tested.
- Identifies alternative providers or in-house solutions.
- Estimates transition timelines, costs and risks.
- Addresses transfer or secure deletion of data.
- Is updated at minimum annually and upon material change.

## 5. Register of Information

5.1. The Company maintains a Register of Information of all contractual arrangements with ICT third-party service providers in the format prescribed by Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2956.

5.2. The Register is updated within ten (10) business days of any new arrangement, material amendment, or termination.

5.3. The Register is submitted annually to the National Competent Authority and made available upon request at any time.

## 6. Sub-Contracting Chains

6.1. The Company shall maintain visibility over the full sub-contracting chain of ICT services supporting CIFs, including:

- Identity, role and location of each sub-contractor.
- Functions or sub-functions performed.
- Material changes to the chain.

6.2. The Company retains the contractual right to object to material changes in the sub-contracting chain that would expose the Company to additional risk.

## 7. Notification to the NCA

7.1. The Company notifies the National Competent Authority in a timely manner of:

- Planned contractual arrangements supporting CIFs.
- Any material change to such arrangements.
- The fact that a function previously not classified as CIF has become a CIF.

## 8. Termination Rights

8.1. The Company shall be entitled to terminate any contractual arrangement, without prejudice to applicable transition periods, in at least the circumstances listed in DORA Art. 28(7), including:

- Significant breach by the provider of applicable laws, regulations or contractual terms.
- Circumstances identified during the ongoing monitoring deemed capable of materially altering the performance of the function.
- Provider's evidenced weaknesses in its overall ICT risk management.
- The competent authority cannot effectively supervise the Company as a result of the arrangement.

## 9. Record-Keeping

9.1. The Company retains for at least five (5) years from termination:

- All contractual documents and amendments.
- Due diligence and risk-assessment files.
- Service-level reports and ongoing monitoring evidence.
- Audit reports and remediation evidence.
- Exit strategy documents and any test results.

## 10. Review

10.1. This Policy is reviewed at least annually, after any material incident involving an ICT third-party service provider, and following entry into force of new Level 2 measures under DORA Chapter V Section I.
